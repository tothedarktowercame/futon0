# Client problems kernel — concrete problems end clients brought to vendors

Compiled 2026-10-10 from vendor case studies, customer stories, press. Vendor-reported numbers are marked. "None found" means no concrete case was located, not that none exists.

## Agentic Fabriq
- **Sells to:** engineering/security teams at companies deploying AI agents internally or embedding them in customer-facing products, especially in regulated industries (YC W26, 2-person team, pre-seed).
- **Client problems found:**
  - none found. No named customers, case studies or testimonials on the site, YC profile, or press; the site uses fictional personas (Dana in Sales, Priya in Support, Alex as Admin). The problem as the founders frame it: teams hardcode a shared API key for agents, the user's identity is lost once an agent acts, and "who did this agent act for?" cannot be answered; site slogans are "One shared key. Too much access." / "Policies in prompts. No enforcement." / "Actions everywhere. No shared record." (vendor framing, not a client). Source: https://agenticfabriq.com ; https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/agentic-fabriq
- **Human vs tooling:** tooling. The intent (least privilege, an audit trail) already exists, but identity infrastructure built for humans can't carry it through to agents.

## Advanced AI Society Proof-of-Control
- **Sells to:** Not a vendor; an open standard (hosted at Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust) aimed at enterprise security/risk leaders, insurers and regulators. Members named are mostly crypto/decentralised-infra organisations (Hedera, NEAR, Filecoin Foundation, Edge & Node).
- **Client problems found:**
  - AstraZeneca (Charles Iheagwara, quoted as a supporter): the company is asked to trust vendors' claims about what an agent did. "We're running on a broken trust model, vendor assertions instead of evidence. That doesn't work when patient safety and our intellectual property are on the line." This is a stated concern, not a named incident. Source: https://thenextweb.com/news/advanced-ai-society-linux-foundation-proof-of-control
  - Financial regulators/markets (J. Christopher Giancarlo, former CFTC chair): agent actions are not backed by "verifiable, audit-ready evidence", which regulation assumes. Stated concern; no named case. Source: https://thenextweb.com/news/advanced-ai-society-linux-foundation-proof-of-control
  - Commercial insurers (described as a group): "over 4,000 generative AI exclusions" have been filed against commercial liability policies, so AI operating risk cannot be insured without verification (figure reported by the Society). Source: https://advancedaisociety.org
  - Enterprises (described as a group): "~42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025", put down to a lack of trust (figure reported by the Society, cited from third parties). Source: https://advancedaisociety.org
  - No concrete deployment or client incident was found. Fortune's coverage cites only frontier-lab agent incidents, not end-client cases. Source: https://fortune.com/2026/10/05/ai-industry-hates-crypto-people-ai-agents-proof-of-control-verification-advanced-ai-society-tricia-wang/
- **Human vs tooling:** Mixed, leaning human/institutional. Enterprises and insurers are unwilling to delegate to agents because there is no evidence they can accept. The proposed fix is tooling (tamper-evident logs).

## Scribe (incl. Scribe Optimize)
- **Sells to:** enterprise ops / continuous-improvement / enablement / CS leaders (insurance, banking, energy, healthcare RCM, manufacturing); Optimize is bought by AI/transformation leads who must produce an AI roadmap or ROI case.
- **Client problems found:**
  - TXNM Energy (utility, ~2,000 staff; Optimize): leadership mandated AI but nobody could see how work was actually done; process discovery meant "large teams stuck in a conference room, sticky notes on the wall" for weeks; vendor-reported 5 days to AI roadmap, 100 hours saved per person per department. Source: https://scribe.com/customers/txnm-energy
  - Compare Club (Australian comparison/insurance broker; Optimize): every automation project had to prove 4x return, but business cases were built from interviews and memory, 2+ weeks each; "proving AI ROI felt like guesswork"; estimates wrong after the fact (30-minute task actually 2 hours) led to weeks of argument; UiPath task-mining tool deprecated so they fell back to interviews; vendor-reported 86% less analyst time, 76 hours of duplicate call-disposition entry found in one task over 4 weeks. Source: https://scribe.com/customers/compare-club
  - Coronis Health (healthcare revenue-cycle management): "My team would sit there taking screenshots and copy/pasting images and text in Word docs ... would waste hours of my team's time"; no single source of truth for SOPs, staff made their own workarounds; procedure compliance 80%, task accuracy 75% (vendor-reported, rising to 98%/95%). Source: https://scribe.com/customers/coronis
  - Northern Trust (bank, 20,000+ staff): hard to capture and spread process knowledge globally; staff not following standard procedures (client-service and compliance risk); pandemic remote training across time zones; procedure updates meant manually typing steps and inserting screenshots; vendor-reported 69% less time on non-client tasks. Source: https://scribe.com/library/case-study-northern-trust
  - Crexi (commercial real-estate marketplace, CS team): answering each client question with screenshots/video took 5-10 minutes; features shipped every two weeks so SharePoint training went stale; manager estimated two months to bring SharePoint up to date; "Before Scribe, we were doing screenshots in Word docs"; vendor-reported 90% less doc-creation time, 70% less time answering client questions. Source: https://scribe.com/customers/crexi
  - Learning Pool (e-learning, 18-person QA team): "It could take an hour to document the steps for a ticket — and that's without screenshots"; inconsistent formats across six products; missing steps meant engineers could not reproduce bugs; vendor-reported 1 hour to 5 minutes per ticket. Source: https://scribe.com/customers/learningpool
  - Crosscard (sales ops/enablement): one how-to article took 10-15+ minutes; vendor-reported 1-2 minutes. Source: https://scribe.com/library/case-study-crosscard
  - Simplot (agri-food), Kubota (manufacturing), New York Life (insurance): listed only as knowledge silos / documentation time / learning across 80+ teams; vendor-reported 93% less time explaining work, 75% less documentation time, 41.6 hours/month saved per employee; no client wording found. Source: https://scribe.com/customers
- **Human vs tooling:** mostly tooling (people already intend to document and follow procedures; writing them is slow and they go stale), but the Optimize cases are mixed: leadership has an AI mandate with no shared picture of the work, and ROI is argued from memory.

## TinyFish
- **Sells to:** Ops, business-intelligence, analytics and product teams at consumer platforms (delivery, travel, insurance comparison, car-sharing, fitness marketplaces) that have to read or act on many third-party websites with no API.
- **Client problems found:**
  - ClassPass (program management, Jonathan Lam): staff logged into partner studios' booking systems by hand to check class times and catch schedule mismatches before members hit them, across 32,000+ partners; 98.6% less manual work, $43k/yr saved, 835 venues automated (vendor-reported). Source: https://www.tinyfish.ai/customers/classpass
  - GetGo, Southeast Asian car-sharing (BI, Cassandra Seow): competitor availability and pricing change through the day, and manual site checks were too slow for pricing decisions. "market insights we couldn't collect manually"; ~100% competitor coverage, multiple updates a day (vendor-reported). Source: https://www.tinyfish.ai/customers/getgo
  - Grubhub (Revenue Analytics, Emily Starr): the merchant scoring model missed locally loved restaurants whose signal sits on Reddit, YouTube and local forums. "We have a lot of data already, but we were missing genuine crowd-sourced sentiment."; 353 net-new restaurants in Boston, 20% cohort expansion (vendor-reported). Source: https://www.tinyfish.ai/customers/grubhub
  - Google Hotels Japan (product lead): 40,000+ small Japanese hotels have no booking-platform integration, only old, fragmented websites, so their rooms were missing from search. "reach supply we couldn't access any other way"; 3M+ impressions/yr, 20-30% search visibility gain (vendor-reported). Source: https://www.tinyfish.ai/customers/google-hotels-japan
  - The Zebra, insurance comparison (Chief AI Officer Daniel Herrington): pulling quotes from many insurer websites took minutes per workflow; "workflows that once took minutes now happen in seconds", quotes now under 2 minutes (vendor-reported; no baseline given). Source: https://www.tinyfish.ai/customers/the-zebra
  - DoorDash: "manages web interaction complexity at scale", 1M+ quarterly web workflows (vendor-reported; no problem detail). Source: https://www.tinyfish.ai/customers
- **Human vs tooling:** Tooling. The intent is clear and already being done (check schedules, watch competitors, collect quotes), but by hand, slowly and incompletely, because the counterpart sites have no API.

## AIUC-1
- **Sells to:** AI-agent vendors who need to get through enterprise security review and procurement (Cursor, Fin/Intercom, ElevenLabs, Harvey, UiPath, Sierra, Lovable, Recraft), plus enterprises certifying their own agents (KPMG, MSCI). AIUC also sells AI-agent liability insurance (reported up to $50M) that relies on the certification.
- **Client problems found:**
  - MSCI (financial data/indices; enterprise certifying its own agent): its SecOps Agent approves privileged-access requests on Sarbanes-Oxley (SOX)-regulated systems, so a single wrong approval is an audit finding. CISO John Rogers: existing governance frameworks did not "answer the question I knew our auditors would ask ... 'does this agent actually do what the policy says, and can you prove it?'" It ran about 12 agents in early 2026, 40+ by summer, and expects hundreds within 18 months; AIUC ran about 1,400 tests across 112 privileged roles ahead of the 2026 SOX audit (vendor-reported). Source: https://www.aiuc-1.com/research/how-msci-secures-its-highest-risk-ai-agents-with-aiuc-1
  - KPMG US (aIQ Capture, an internal agentic platform): wanted independent proof before putting the firm's name behind its own agent. "Before the firm put its name behind our own agentic system, we had it independently tested"; 900+ tests for hallucination, prompt injection and similar risks (vendor-reported). Source: https://kpmg.com/us/en/media/news/kpmg-llp-becomes-first-big-four-firm-with-aiuc-1-certified-ai-capability.html
  - Lovable (app builder selling to enterprises): enterprise deals stalled at security sign-off, not on whether the product worked. "The barrier there has never really been whether the software functions. It is whether a security team signs off." Its non-technical builders (about 80% of users) had nothing to hand to reviewers, apps used a single shared credential, and nobody was clearly liable for an agent's actions; it added AIUC-1 plus a Lloyd's-backed insurance policy. Source: https://thenextweb.com/news/lovable-aiuc-1-trust-centers-enterprise-procurement
  - ElevenLabs (voice agents): enterprise customers deploying its voice agents wanted someone to carry the liability; it was the first AIUC-1-backed insurance policy, underwriting agent actions for ElevenLabs' customers. Co-founder: certification helps "companies deploy at scale with confidence" (vendor-reported). Source: https://bridgemena.com/news/elevenlabs-launches-the-worlds-first-insurance-policy-for-ai-voice-agents-backed-by-aiuc-1 ; https://www.aiuc-1.com
  - Fin/Intercom (support agent): needed to separate itself from competitors' claims about security. "Competitors talk about security. We're putting our product through quarterly independent testing – and we're willing to show the results." Source: https://www.aiuc-1.com
  - UiPath (automation agents): 2,000+ evaluations (vendor-reported); head of GRC (governance, risk and compliance): "the testing produced new guardrails, and we can show customers exactly what improved." Source: https://itbrief.news/story/uipath-secures-landmark-aiuc-1-certification-for-ai-agents ; https://www.aiuc-1.com
  - Cursor, Harvey: testimonials only ("the only standard that keeps up with our pace"; trust as "a continuous commitment"). No concrete problem stated. Source: https://www.aiuc-1.com
  - Critique (independent): AIUC writes the standard, runs the tests, issues the certificates and sells the insurance, a conflict of interest the author compares to credit ratings. Source: https://zeltser.com/aiuc-1-cert
- **Human vs tooling:** mixed, mostly human. The buyer's security team or auditor won't sign off without proof and someone liable, so the gap is trust and accountability between organisations; the testing itself is the tooling part.

## Tatras Data (tatrasdata.com)
- **Sells to:** operations/finance/claims/marketing heads at mid-to-large enterprises (insurance, banking, retail, manufacturing, industrial water treatment, proptech, public-sector platforms); US-headquartered (Mount Pleasant, SC) with India delivery. Clients are anonymised in all ~50 published stories.
- **Client problems found:**
  - Global insurer ("one of the world's largest"): thousands of daily complaint tickets (delayed claims, ambiguous policy terms); staff manually read, classify, cross-reference prior tickets/policies, route, and write regulator-compliant replies; result 70% faster resolution, 40% fewer escalations (vendor-reported). Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/using-genai-to-automate-customer-complaint-resolution-for-a-global-insurance-provider/
  - Vehicle insurer: hail-damage assessment by expert adjusters looking at photos was slow, subjective, inconsistent, missed small dents, and backed up at high claim volume; no numbers given. Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/ai-powered-hail-damage-identification-using-computer-vision/
  - Unnamed client finance team: thousands of physical invoices weekly, each in a different format; analysts manually extracted amounts and hunted for price mismatches against contract clauses stored in separate documents/systems; 90%+ mismatch-detection accuracy (vendor-reported). Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/smarter-invoice-to-contract-reconciliation-with-ai-powered-document-parsing/
  - Financial institution (credit underwriting): underwriters manually checked scanned bank statements for minimum balance, recurring transactions and 3+ months of activity; labour-intensive, inconsistent, costly, with strict privacy constraints; "turnaround time dropped" (vendor-reported, no figure). Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/ai-powered-document-vetting-for-credit-risk-using-open-source-llms/
  - Major Middle East electronics retailer: store managers could not predict daily footfall (Monday swung 120-500) or link ad spend to store visits, so they over- or under-staffed and launched promotions blind; 17% lower staff costs, 14% better marketing efficiency (vendor-reported). Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/forecasting-store-footfall-with-neural-networks-for-smarter-staffing-promotions/
  - Water-purification equipment company: when a client system failed, field account managers and support staff dug through 200-page OEM manuals with Ctrl+F; new recruits did not know where to look, veterans skimmed five manuals per question; "dramatic reduction in resolution time" (vendor-reported). Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/faster-field-support-in-engineering-with-llms-technical-document-parsing/
  - Industrial reverse-osmosis operator: corrosion coupons collected weekly, inspected under a microscope, sent to a lab, 7+ days before treatment could be adjusted; 7 days to 5 minutes, 95% classification accuracy (vendor-reported). Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/automating-metal-corrosion-detection-with-ai-powered-visual-classification/
  - Proptech firm: every property inspection report arrived as a differently formatted PDF (some handwritten), and the ops team mapped each one by hand to repair tasks and cost estimates; 65% drop in manual effort (vendor-reported). Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/reducing-errors-manual-effort-in-document-processing-using-vision-and-nlp/
  - Large industrial enterprises (incident investigation): after factory-floor incidents, supervisors worked from paper forms, phone calls and incomplete witness statements, so root causes were found late and incidents repeated; no figures. Source: https://tatrasdata.com/portfolio/ai-powered-incident-analysis-reduced-investigation-time-and-improved-root-cause-accuracy/
- **Human vs tooling:** Tooling. In every case the client already wanted the outcome (resolve complaints, reconcile invoices, staff correctly) and was doing it by hand from unstructured documents, slowly and inconsistently.

## Hyperbound
- **Sells to:** sales enablement / sales training directors and VPs of sales at outbound-heavy sales orgs (B2B SaaS, staffing, offshore staffing, fintech); buyers are the people responsible for new-hire ramp.
- **Client problems found:**
  - ALKU (US IT/life-sciences staffing firm, 300+ sellers in an internship-style program): reps made hundreds of calls but connected with only a handful, so they got too few real conversations to learn from; not enough managers to run practice as the program grew to hundreds; "Reps weren't struggling because they weren't working hard. They were struggling because they didn't have enough chances to practice."; vendor-reported time to first deal 6 months to 3. Source: https://hyperbound.ai/blog/how-alku-cut-time-to-first-deal-in-half-by-scaling-repetition
  - Staff Domain (offshore staffing, sales teams in Australia and the Philippines): CEO: "You can make 100 calls a day and speak to 5 people. If you're not sharp in those moments, you won't get past 'hello.'"; uneven performance across countries; took 90 days to spot who needed coaching; inconsistent objection handling; vendor-reported coaching needs spotted in 4 weeks, 300% more meetings. Source: https://hyperbound.ai/blog/staff-domain
  - JumpCloud (IT software, ~90 reps): enablement lead typed buyer profiles by hand for each roleplay ("It might take me an hour, hour and a half"); a 45-minute session served only a few reps, plus 20-30 minutes feedback each; one round for all 90 reps would take 11 workdays; "as our team got bigger, we couldn't continue to scale that"; vendor-reported 2x faster time to first meeting. Source: https://www.hyperbound.ai/blog/2x-faster-time-to-first-meeting-how-jumpcloud-scaled-sales-training
  - finally (US SMB accounting/finance platform, SDR team): "You had to be present to practice ... managers were the biggest constraint"; SDRs could not sound the same "at 9:30 in the morning on a Monday as you do at 4:30 in the afternoon on a Thursday"; vendor-reported 50% faster ramp, 2x meeting volume. Source: https://www.hyperbound.ai/blog/finally
  - Nivoda (B2B diamond marketplace): had to onboard 30 new reps without putting revenue at risk; vendor-reported 50% shorter ramp, 30% more demos booked. Source: https://www.casestudies.com/company/hyperbound/case-study/how-nivoda-onboarded-30-reps-without-risking-revenue
  - Vanta (compliance software): scaling the SDR/BDR team 4x without diluting training; vendor-reported ramp 210 days to 72 days (60% faster). No client wording found. Source: https://www.hyperbound.ai/success-stories
  - Klaviyo (marketing software, 1,000+ go-to-market reps): consistent training at scale without loading managers with coaching; vendor-reported +42% new-hire productivity, 7 hours/week saved per manager. No client wording found. Source: https://www.hyperbound.ai/success-stories
- **Human vs tooling:** tooling, constrained by scarce people: reps and managers want to practise, but practice depends on manager hours and on rare live connects; the shortage of real conversations is structural (low pickup rates), not a matter of motivation.

## o-machine
- **Sells to:** invite-only beta. The 2026 beta call targeted "private market investor[s]" and autonomous-driving professionals; it also seeks contacts in supply chain and defence. Its market-simulation product "oraclea" is "deployed with select enterprise partners" (CPG, i.e. consumer packaged goods, portfolios). Much of its public material is benchmarks against Claude and Gemini, not customer stories.
- **Client problems found:**
  - Major consumer brand, unnamed ("a leading CPG portfolio"): it was about to shift its portfolio toward health food on the industry consensus that GLP-1 drugs mean people eat fewer calories. In the case study's words it "knew this was likely oversimplified, but they were flying blind": surveys capture what people think they should say, and demographic models average the market out, so it was "preparing to make major financial decisions based on a hunch." The vendor says a simulation of 5,000 synthetic consumers found the fastest adopters are high-income, non-obese people adopting for cultural reasons, and that they were leaving one of the brand's most profitable premium categories, one nobody linked to weight loss (vendor-reported, client unnamed, category redacted). Source: https://o-machine.com/case-studies/ozempic-blind-spot
  - Unnamed robotics prototype (an internal test, not clearly a client): detecting physical threats (payload drop, collision, friction, evasion) from a 6-DOF robotic arm's telemetry, reported at 15 microseconds on a CPU (vendor-reported). Source: https://o-machine.com
