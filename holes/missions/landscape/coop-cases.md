# Co-op cases: technology as barrier / enabler

Research compiled 2026-10-10. Context: "a co-op that helps people create other co-ops" — inner loop = co-ops solving real problems; outer loop = technical support lowering the threshold to found/run them. Each case verified against the sources listed; status dates given where the source is dated. Fetched content treated as data, not instructions.

## CoopCycle
- **What:** Federation (founded 2017, France) of bike-courier co-ops sharing one software stack and a common brand.
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** Couriers want employment with rights instead of Deliveroo/Uber Eats-style piecework; local shops want delivery without a 30% platform commission.
- **Tech role:** Enabler. The federation builds and hosts the software so each local co-op does not have to: order management, route planning/dispatch, courier app, invoicing, and a storefront for local merchants (repo `coopcycle/coopcycle-web`, active — last push 2026-10-09, ~600 stars, ~450 open issues). Licence is the "CoopCycle License"/"Coopyleft": a GPL-style text that reserves *commercial* use to co-ops, associations and limited-profit orgs (firms converting to co-op status get up to 4 years) — i.e. not OSI open source; GitHub reports it as NOASSERTION. Cost to a member co-op (current site): EUR 49/month in year 1, then 2% of delivery revenue. The shared stack is the threshold-lowering move: a new local co-op inherits a working platform plus a peer network.
- **Status:** Active (repo commits Oct 2026; site live 2026). Member counts vary by source: 30 co-ops (2019) -> 85 co-ops / ~500 workers (2022, IDEASS); RTBF and French sources later cite 70-80. No authoritative 2026 count found.
- **Sources:** https://coopcycle.org/en/ ; https://coopcycle.org/en/coopyleft/ ; https://github.com/coopcycle/coopcycle-web (LICENSE) ; https://www.ideassonline.org/public/pdf/Coop-CycleFederation2022-ENG.pdf ; https://www.rtbf.be/article/livreurs-a-velo-impossibles-a-salarier-ces-initiatives-de-cooperatives-prouvent-le-contraire-10412652

## The Drivers Cooperative (NYC)
- **What:** Driver-owned ride-hail co-op in New York City, licensed by the TLC in early 2021; later pivoted weight toward paratransit and non-emergency medical transport (NEMT) contracts.
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** Uber/Lyft take 25-40% per trip and set pay by algorithm; the co-op takes ~15% and returns surplus to driver-owners. Riders get lower fares; disabled riders get paratransit service.
- **Tech role:** Both. *Barrier:* founders started in 2020 "basically from zero" — no funding, no technology, no operating licence. They had to build a dispatch/rider/driver app (hosted on Google Cloud), obtain a TLC base licence, and write custom code for paratransit contracts so drivers are guaranteed minimum wage ("Co-op 2.0" platform). Capital was the binding constraint: crowdfunding, and co-founder Erik Forman personally guaranteed a $400,000 line of credit and donated years of labour. The Workers Lab names "absence of infrastructure supporting worker-owned tech businesses" as what makes this inaccessible to workers on 60+ hour weeks. *Enabler:* once built, the platform let the co-op bid for institutional (paratransit/NEMT) contracts, not only compete on consumer rides. **Spin-off evidence:** Drivers Cooperative Colorado launched September 2024, **suspended operations in April 2025 because of "technical issues with its original app"**, and relaunched 16 July 2025 on a white-label app ("COOP Rideshare") licensed from TADA, a Singapore ride-hail firm — TADA owns the tech and charges a flat ~$0.60/ride rather than a commission. Other driver groups (Minnesota, Illinois, Virginia) reportedly enquired about using the same tech.
- **Status:** NYC: active per drivers.coop (Oct 2026; iOS/Android app live, NYC service page; emphasis on paratransit/NEMT). Driver counts in sources: ~2,500 signed up (2020-21); Workers Lab (July 2024) cites growth to 12,000 by year two — the figures are not reconciled. Colorado: active on TADA stack as of July 2025 relaunch.
- **Sources:** https://drivers.coop/ ; https://theworkerslab.com/updates/innovator-drivers-cooperative (July 2024) ; https://www.npr.org/transcripts/943454048 (2020) ; https://www.denver7.com/news/local-news/drivers-cooperative-colorado-relaunches-with-new-app-and-expanded-services (July 2025) ; https://www.westword.com/news/colorado-driver-owned-rideshare-app-relaunching-summer-bugs-24177145/ ; https://www.westword.com/news/colorado-driver-owned-ridershare-app-relaunches-improvements-25058418

## Up & Go (NYC)
- **What:** Online booking platform for home/office cleaning, launched May 2017, owned by the worker co-ops that use it (initially several Brooklyn/NYC cleaning co-ops incubated by the Center for Family Life in Sunset Park).
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** Immigrant women cleaners in small co-ops could not compete with Handy-type apps on discoverability, booking and payment; the platform gives them online booking while keeping ~95% of the price (5% funds the platform, vs 20-50% taken by mainstream gig platforms). Members report ~25% above local average hourly pay.
- **Tech role:** Enabler, but grant-dependent. The co-ops could not build it themselves: the site was co-designed with workers and built by CoLab Cooperative (a tech worker co-op), with guidance from Cornell Tech and funding from the Robin Hood Foundation and Barclays. CoLab continues to add functionality as needed. Members own the brand and the code. The model has been replicated to Philadelphia and trialled in Detroit — i.e. a shared platform reused by new local co-ops, the same pattern as CoopCycle.
- **Status:** Active as far as visible: upandgo.coop live (footer "© 2024"); Platform Co-op Directory entry updated 8 Mar 2024 lists 50 workers / 49 members, "Phase 3: Growth". No 2025-26 news of closure or growth found.
- **Sources:** https://colab.coop/work/upandgo/ ; https://home.barclays/news/2017/09/up-and-go-allows-us-to-compete (Sept 2017) ; https://directory.platform.coop/organizations/1832 (Mar 2024) ; https://www.upandgo.coop/ ; https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Up_and_Go

## Cooperative Home Care Associates (CHCA, Bronx)
- **What:** Worker-owned licensed home care agency (founded 1985 by Rick Surpin); repeatedly described as the largest worker co-op in the US (~2,000 workers in 2018 coverage); B Corp; aides are also 1199SEIU members.
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** Home health aides — mostly women of colour and immigrants — get training, more stable hours, an ownership stake ($1,000 share paid by ~$3.65/week payroll deduction) and board seats (8 of 13); elderly/disabled clients in the Bronx get Medicaid-funded care with lower aide turnover.
- **Tech role:** Mostly barrier, via the payer system rather than via software CHCA chose. Revenue is overwhelmingly Medicaid, so the co-op lives inside state rules: (a) the shift to Medicaid managed long-term care (MLTC) meant CHCA must contract with plans and accept fewer reimbursed hours; it once got ~60% of business through a sister MLTC plan, Independence Care System (ICS), which **ceased operations 31 Mar 2019** (5,350 members moved to VNSNY CHOICE); (b) federal 21st Century Cures Act **electronic visit verification (EVV)**: NY requires it for personal care since 1 Jan 2021 and home health since 1 Jan 2023, under a "choice model" where **each provider selects and pays for its own EVV system** and submits data to DOH; (c) clearinghouse/billing software is described in trade press as expensive for small agencies. I found **no CHCA-specific source** describing its scheduling/billing stack or costs — the burden above is sector-level and inferred to apply. Low reimbursement plus COVID costs led to job losses (Harvard public-health coverage). Not an enabler story; the threshold here is compliance infrastructure that every new home-care co-op would have to replicate.
- **Status:** Active (chcany.org live Oct 2026: Medicaid, private pay, LTC insurance, VA; HHA/PCA training programmes). No current worker count published on the site.
- **Sources:** https://www.chcany.org/ ; https://nonprofitquarterly.org/employee-ownerships-presence-grows-home-healthcare/ (Apr 2018) ; https://homehealthcarenews.com/2015/09/can-employee-ownership-end-the-home-health-wage-wars/ ; https://cleo.rutgers.edu/articles/cooperative-home-care-associates/ ; https://leadingageny.org/providers/home-and-community-based-services/mltc-and-medicaid-redesign/independence-care-system-to-cease-operations/ ; https://frontline.vnshealth.org/choice-mltc-welcomes-over-5300-new-members ; https://osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2024/11/13/medicaid-program-provider-compliance-electronic-visit-verification-program (Nov 2024) ; https://homecaremag.com/september-2022/so-you-want-bill-medicaid

## Smart (Belgium; "Smart Coop")
- **What:** "Shared enterprise" for freelancers: founded 1998 as a non-profit for artists, became a co-operative (SmartCoop) in 2017; members invoice through Smart and are paid as its employees.
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** Freelancers/creatives with irregular gigs lack social insurance and face admin (VAT, payroll, social security, tax declarations) and late-paying clients. With Smart they find their own clients and set prices, but Smart is the employer: it invoices, pays wages **7 days after the work is done even if the client hasn't paid**, handles social-security and tax filings, shares one VAT number, provides insurance; individual bankruptcy is impossible.
- **Tech role:** Enabler — the admin-absorption is delivered through a shared online management platform (order forms, invoicing, contracts, payroll, expenses) operated centrally; members don't need their own legal entity or accounting tools. Cost to member: EUR 30 share per year + fixed 6.5% of turnover + variable ~8% employment-budget contribution. *Barrier side (regulatory, not software):* Smart France lost its ability to run event-production activity after an administrative sanction in Oct 2020, losing ~4,000 intermittent workers and ~50% of revenue, followed by a painful restructuring (Politis, Mar 2022).
- **Status:** Active (Belgian 2024 user brochure published Nov 2025; statutes amended at 2026 AGM). >20,000 workers used Smart in Belgium in 2022; 9 Belgian cities; partner network in 7 European countries (older sources give 9 countries / 40 cities).
- **Sources:** https://smartbe.be/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Smart_plaquette_users_en_2024_web.pdf ; https://cecop.coop/stories/smart-belgium ; https://european-social-fund-plus.ec.europa.eu/en/social-innovation-match/case-study/smart-coop ; https://www.politis.fr/articles/2022/03/smart-les-deboires-de-la-cooperative-anti-uberisation-44143/ ; https://new.smartbe.be/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Statuts-de-SmartCoop-Versions-comparees-AG2026.pdf

## Driver's Seat Cooperative (gig-worker data co-op)
- **What:** Driver-owned data co-op (Portland, OR; co-founder Hays Witt) whose app, launched 2019, let ride-hail/delivery workers pool their own trip and earnings data.
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** Uber/Lyft/DoorDash pay is opaque; drivers can't see true hourly earnings after expenses or the platform's take. Pooled data told members when/where to work, and gave worker groups (Rideshare Drivers United, Los Deliveristas Unidos, Colorado Independent Drivers Union) evidence for pay-transparency campaigns; the co-op also aimed to sell aggregated data to cities and share revenue with members.
- **Tech role:** Both. *Enabler:* the app was the co-op — no app, no data. *Barrier:* a small co-op could not keep a data-scraping app current against platforms that change constantly; a 2025 Princeton thesis states "the app has lacked regular updates, faces usability challenges, and no longer fully meets the evolving needs of its users." The software outlived the co-op form: the app was **transferred to the Workers' Algorithm Observatory (WAO), a Princeton CITP research group**, and is now distributed as "WAO Driver's Seat" (free). WAO's sibling tool FairFare (2025) crowdsources fare data to estimate the platform "take rate", built with a Colorado rideshare union.
- **Status:** Co-operative effectively inactive — driversseat.co shows "Squarespace — Website Expired" (checked 2026-10-10); no dissolution notice found. App survives under university stewardship (WAO, App Store listing current; CITP news 2025).
- **Sources:** https://wao.cs.princeton.edu/ ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/drivers-seat/id6478721051 ; https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/7c5e874b-3fd3-4a82-80f3-5ff45e6210c1/full (Apr 2025) ; https://citp.princeton.edu/news/2025/new-tool-drives-gig-workers-toward-wage-transparency ; https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/gig-workers-find-solidarity-through-data ; https://directory.platform.coop/organizations/1825

## Co-op Cloud / Autonomic
- **What:** "Public interest infrastructure… an alternative to corporate clouds built by tech co-ops": a shared packaging + deployment toolkit for self-hosting libre apps (Nextcloud, Jitsi, MediaWiki, Rocket.Chat…), started by Autonomic Co-operative (UK worker co-op) and now run as a federation.
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** Small co-ops, unions and community groups depend on Google/Microsoft/Slack, or on one fragile volunteer's server. Co-op Cloud lets small tech co-ops host apps for such clients from shared, maintained "recipes" instead of each reinventing deployment.
- **Tech role:** Enabler for tech co-ops, still a barrier for non-technical ones. Components: `abra` (CLI "utility belt" that deploys/upgrades/backs up apps), recipes (Docker Compose files per app; ~247 repos in the coop-cloud org), Docker Swarm + Traefik underneath — Swarm chosen as "most suitable for small technology" over Kubernetes; one database per app for isolation (vs YunoHost's shared services). Copyleft. Self-declared limits: "still beta quality software", x86-only, CLI-dependent, requires container/Compose knowledge; target users are "tech co-ops, collectives and individuals who have familiarity with system administration". It lowers the threshold for *hosting providers*, not for the end co-op. Funded by member dues and grants (European Cultural Foundation among co-funders); members/backers include IWW, Servers.coop, United Tech and Allied Workers, ruangrupa/lumbung.space.
- **Status:** Active: abra repo updated 2026-10-09; latest release 0.13.0-beta (23 Feb 2026); docs footer "Copyleft 2020-2025"; autonomic.zone site live.
- **Sources:** https://coopcloud.tech/ ; https://docs.coopcloud.tech/ ; https://docs.coopcloud.tech/intro/faq/ ; https://docs.coopcloud.tech/federation/ ; https://git.coopcloud.tech/toolshed/abra ; https://autonomic.zone/

## Co-op developers / incubators (Start.coop, Platform Cooperativism Consortium, Project Equity, Democracy at Work Institute)
These are the existing "co-ops that help create co-ops" (or non-profits that do). None of them primarily ships shared operational software to members; the tech offer is thin.

### Start.coop (US)
- **What:** Non-profit co-op incubator/accelerator (first US co-op accelerator; inaugural cohort ~2018-19).
- **Problem it addresses:** Founders lack business coaching, capital and cooperative-specific legal setup.
- **Tech role:** Minor. Offers incubation (pre-revenue), acceleration (post-revenue), consulting and fiscal sponsorship; builds a co-op assessment tool, educational modules and "three templatized legal forms". Fall 2025 accelerator (4 Sep-5 Dec 2025, Black-led co-ops in US/Canada): $5,000 grant, weekly workshops, 8 coaching sessions, on-demand modules, "curated discounts on essential platform services and business management tools (e.g., legal, accounting, CRM, marketing)". Earlier cohorts used revenue-based financing (~$10k invested, small revenue share repaid). Tech appears only as discounted SaaS, not as built infrastructure. No published count of co-ops served on the homepage.
- **Status:** Active (2025 cohort).
- **Sources:** https://start.coop/ ; https://www.runfutureproof.com/acceleratorhub/programs/fall-2025-accelerator ; https://ncbaclusa.coop/blog/start-coop-first-u-s-co-op-accelerator-launches-its-inaugural-cohort-of-startups/

### Platform Cooperativism Consortium (PCC, The New School, NYC; Trebor Scholz)
- **What:** Research/advocacy hub and network for platform co-ops; runs conferences (2024 Mombasa, 2025 Istanbul), the Institute for the Cooperative Digital Economy (ICDE) fellowships, and a directory/resource library.
- **Problem it addresses:** Says it is "approached almost daily" by groups wanting to start a platform co-op who don't know how.
- **Tech role:** Attempted enabler. The **Platform Co-op Development Kit** ($1M Google.org grant, July 2018-July 2020, built with OCAD's Inclusive Design Research Centre) promised open-source labour platforms and governance tools, a learning commons, curricula, a co-op map and legal network, piloted with 5 groups (3,000 Illinois childcare workers; SEWA beauty workers, Ahmedabad; Brazilian waste pickers; refugee women in Hamburg; Australian home-care workers). Delivered outputs that are visible today are mainly knowledge infrastructure: the Resource Library, a Tool Library, the Platform Co-op Directory, and "Pinecone" (a web component library). PCC itself stated success "can only be measured upon completion"; I found **no published outcome report** with numbers on co-ops launched. Current framing (Dec 2025): "The Solidarity Stack" — co-op data centres, AI co-ops, federated infrastructure, small LLMs — a vision, not a product.
- **Status:** Active (2025-26 blog, events, ICDE cohorts).
- **Sources:** https://platform.coop/blog/the-platform-co-op-development-kit/ ; https://www.shareable.net/platform-cooperativism-consortium-awarded-1-million-google-org-grant/ ; https://floeproject.org/news/2020-07-15-platform-co-op-update ; https://platform.coop/blog/the-solidarity-stack/ ; https://directory.platform.coop/

### Project Equity (US, Oakland)
- **What:** Non-profit that helps retiring owners sell to employees (worker co-op, ESOP or Employee Ownership Trust).
- **Problem it addresses:** "Silver tsunami": ~2.9M US businesses with owners 55+ (32.1M employees); most have no exit plan, so they close rather than sell.
- **Tech role:** None stated. Services are advisory end-to-end (exploration -> feasibility -> transition planning -> financing/closing -> post-transition "Thrive" programme), sliding-scale fees. Named barriers are financing, complexity/awareness and succession timing — not software. Scale: 30+ completed transitions since 2017, 100+ businesses served (services page); earlier claim of 1,100+ businesses counselled, 15,000+ stakeholders educated. Note the small conversion count vs the size of the opportunity: transactions are labour-intensive.
- **Status:** Active (2025-26 events).
- **Sources:** https://project-equity.org/services/ ; https://impactalpha.com/how-project-equity-is-turning-employees-into-owners-by-surfing-the-silver-tsunami/

### Democracy at Work Institute (DAWI) + US Federation of Worker Cooperatives (USFWC)
- **What:** DAWI, created by the USFWC, does sector research (biennial census), developer training, and the School for Democratic Management (free online learning platform).
- **Problem it addresses:** Worker co-ops in marginalised communities lack management know-how and support networks.
- **Tech role / evidence:** The 2025 State of the Sector (FY2024 data; 242 respondents; published 2026) is the best numeric evidence on needs. Top needs: affordable health insurance 40%, **marketing 39%**, business/strategic planning 37%, other benefits 37%, **"keeping up with administrative activities" 36%**. Funding sources: pandemic relief (EIDL/PPP) 36%, private grants 35%, loans from worker-owners 31%. Supporting orgs: local co-op networks 39%, co-op developers 32%, accountants 29%, legal providers 22%. Sector size: 820 firms identified (~13,520 workers), estimated ~1,300 firms / 16,000 workers; +34% firms since 2020; 29% began as conversions; 47% of conversions now worker-driven (up from 40%). Technology/software is **not** a named category — admin and marketing burden is where it shows up.
- **Status:** Active.
- **Sources:** https://eohub.rutgers.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-STATE-OF-THE-SECTOR-2.pdf ; https://institute.coop/ ; https://www.thenews.coop/worker-co-ops-rising-in-the-usa/ ; https://usworker.coop/census

## Stocksy United / Resonate / Loomio (one line each)
- **Stocksy United** (Victoria, Canada; 2012; multi-stakeholder co-op of photographers/filmmakers, staff and founders): built its own curated stock-licensing marketplace plus an online member-resolution/voting process for members in 65+ countries; pays artists 50-75% of each licence (vs ~20% industry) and has reported $50.1M cumulative royalties; removed its 1,000-artist cap to grow (date of change not confirmed; GEO/GDUSA coverage). **Succeeded; active 2026.** Founders came from iStockphoto, i.e. the tech know-how was imported, not built from scratch. Sources: https://www.stocksy.com/ideas/?p=6290 ; https://geo.coop/content/stocksy-united-removes-its-membership-cap ; https://gdusa.com/stocksy-member-owners-open-door-to-new-contributors/ ; https://betakit.com/?p=32355
- **Resonate** (Berlin; multi-stakeholder music-streaming co-op; launched 2015-17): built a "stream-to-own" player (price rises per play until the 9th play owns the track) and later volunteer-maintained open-source GPL-3.0 code (`resonatecoop/api`, `beam`, last commits Jan 2023); raised $1M from Reflective Ventures (2018). **Failed: resonate.coop now states it operated 2017-2024 and "as of 2026, the co-op is officially closed."** A case where the co-op could not sustain the software it depended on. Sources: https://resonate.coop/ ; https://github.com/resonatecoop ; https://djmag.com/news/relaunched-music-sharing-platform-%E2%80%98resonate%E2%80%99-operates-%E2%80%9Cstream-own%E2%80%9D-model
- **Loomio** (Wellington, NZ; worker co-op spun out of Enspiral/Occupy, 2012): builds AGPL-3.0 group decision-making software (discussions, proposals, polls), sold as SaaS with free self-hosting; repo `loomio/loomio` active (push 2026-10-09; ~2.6k stars; v3.x 2025). **Succeeded modestly and sustainably** — small team (~8 people per third-party profiles; revenue ~US$0.9M per Latka, unverified). It is itself a piece of outer-loop infrastructure: many co-ops use it for governance. Sources: https://github.com/loomio/loomio ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loomio ; https://www.loomio.com/d/mUOKA6vc/long-term-financial-sustainability-for-loomio ; https://getlatka.com/companies/loomio.com

## Farmer/food co-ops and food hubs vs FDA FSMA Section 204 (Food Traceability Rule)
- **What:** Federal record-keeping rule (final rule Nov 2022) for foods on the Food Traceability List (leafy greens, tomatoes, melons, cucumbers, peppers, fresh herbs, shell eggs, soft cheeses, finfish, etc.): seven Critical Tracking Events (harvest, cooling, initial packing, first land-based receiving, shipping, receiving, transformation), each with Key Data Elements and a traceability lot code; firms must give FDA an **electronic sortable spreadsheet within 24 hours** on request.
- **Problem it addresses (for members/clients):** For a farmer co-op, food hub or co-op grocer, the rule is a compliance burden, not a service: any farm selling FTL produce through a food hub, restaurant, retailer or wholesaler is likely covered unless 100% of sales are direct-to-consumer (NSAC). The opportunity: shared traceability record-keeping is exactly the kind of back-office function a co-op or co-op-developer could provide for small members.
- **Tech role:** Barrier (cost of records/systems). **Compliance date:** originally 20 Jan 2026; FDA announced 20 Mar 2025 it would extend 30 months; Congress then directed FDA not to enforce before **20 July 2028** (FY2026 continuing appropriations act, Nov 2025); FDA proposed matching the compliance date. The stated reason: suppliers, "small and mid-size producers, co-packers and distributors", couldn't stand up compliant electronic systems in time. **Exemptions:** farms averaging <=$25,000/yr produce sales (3-yr average); shell-egg producers with <3,000 hens; direct-to-consumer sales; 501(c)(3) non-profits; partial exemptions for some farm-to-school/institution and farm-to-retail sales. Exempt farms still get records demanded contractually by downstream buyers. **Cost evidence:** FDA's final RIA — 323,872 firms / 484,124 establishments covered; annualised domestic cost **$570M** (2020$, 7%, 20 yrs; range $63M-$2.3B). IFDA (distributors, 2025): up to ~$10M one-time for a mid/large distributor, $0.34-$1.00 per case annually. **Small-farm voice:** NSAC (Dec 2020) warned impact "could still be significant for small, sustainable farms"; CAFF (Feb 2021): "for a family farm selling to a local grocery store… with limited means, few staff and hardly an hour to spare, it's a recipe for disaster." At FDA's June 2026 public meeting smaller operators (independent grocers, distributors, smaller producers) cited technology investment, labour, training and ongoing cost. **I found no source quantifying cost specifically for farmer co-ops or food hubs** — the co-op-specific evidence is advocacy, not measurement.
- **Status:** Rule in force; enforcement not before 20 Jul 2028 (as of Oct 2026); FDA guidance still being developed (June 2026 meeting).
- **Sources:** https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods ; https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/economic-impact-analyses-fda-regulations/requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods-final-rule-regulatory-impact-analysis ; https://sustainableagriculture.net/blog/fsma-traceability-rule-part-1 (Dec 2020) ; https://caff.org/unfair-foodsafety-rules/ (Feb 2021) ; https://ifdaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/4-IFDA-Comments-on-RUF-Traceability-Rule-Roundtables-Final-1.pdf ; https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/07/fda-food-traceability-rule-public-meeting (June/July 2026) ; https://www.marlerblog.com/case-news/congress-told-the-fda-to-write-the-food-traceability-rule-by-january-2013-enforcement-now-begins-in-july-2028-here-is-how-fifteen-and-a-half-years-went-by/

## AI / agents / no-code used to bootstrap co-ops (2024-2026)
- **What:** Search for named cases where co-ops or co-op developers used AI, agents, no-code or "vibe coding" to found or run co-ops.
- **Finding (headline):** **No named, documented case found of a co-op or co-op developer using AI coding agents / vibe coding to build its operating software.** Extended searches (co-op press, PCC, CWCF, Co-operatives UK, USFWC, no-code marketplace builders) turned up advice pieces, AI-policy discussions and feasibility studies, not shipped bootstraps. Nearest concrete items:
  - **Co-op Buddy (BCCM, Australia)** — AI chatbot launched **25 Feb 2025** on bccm.coop to answer questions on setting up and running a co-op/mutual (capital raising, constitutional changes); trained on ~100 key publications (co-op manuals, legal/tax docs, research); built on a pilot with **Co-operative Business NZ**. No usage figures or model/vendor disclosed. This is the clearest "AI lowers the founding threshold" example, but it is an advice layer, not infrastructure. Sources: https://bccm.coop/meet-the-new-ai-co-op-tool/ ; https://bccm.coop/co-op-buddy/
  - **Hypha + CanTrust (Canadian worker co-ops)** — feasibility study (reported 13 Aug 2025) for a "co-operative values-driven" AI stack: locally run models with RAG over each co-op's own documents, data kept inside the co-op; funded by The Co-operators' Co-operative Development Program; still early stage. Source: https://canadianworker.coop/co-op-ai-aims-to-offer-an-alternative-to-corporate-platforms/
  - **Baseline (Quebec solidarity co-op) / ai.coop** — AI consultancy for co-ops (training, workshops, custom builds, "seven questions" governance framework); claims AI is entering credit unions, mutual insurers, ag and worker co-ops but names none. Source: https://ai.coop/
  - **OpenCourier (Princeton CITP, Nov 2025-2026)** — open protocol + open-source reference app/back-end/admin dashboard so worker-owned delivery platforms can interoperate (directory, common courier messaging, transparent order management). Research-built shared infrastructure, same lab that now stewards Driver's Seat. Sources: https://opencourier.cs.princeton.edu ; https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02455v1 ; https://citp.princeton.edu/news/2026/citp-researchers-introduce-new-protocol-enable-decentralized-ecosystem-community-owned

## Research: why platform co-ops fail or stall
- **What:** Published evidence on failure/stall causes.
- **Key findings:**
  - **Tortorici, "Mapping Platform Cooperatives", Euricse WP 138/25 (Dec 2025)** — billed as the first international quantitative survey: 86 respondents across six continents, 27 active platform co-ops analysed in depth. Challenge scores: **limited access to finance 18.48 (first choice for 15)**, limited markets/demand/user involvement 15.18, limited member participation 9.57, problems in democratic participation/strategy 3.96, management 0.99. **41% hit legal/regulatory barriers** — the example quoted is a driver co-op: "a significant barrier we faced was to acquire a regulatorily compliant app platform… strict regulatory approval… to ensure our app calculates distance and waiting time… to calculate fares." Capital raised since founding: 14 co-ops $0-100k, 6 $100k-500k, 4 $500k-1M, 3 >$1M; main sources worker-members and grants, then donations/crowdfunding; **almost none from banks or VCs**. Board elected by members in 66%. Appendix C lists **64 platform co-ops identified as failed or inactive** — heavily delivery/courier (e.g. Belo Livraison, Coursiers Wallon, Les Coursiers Stéphanois, Olvo, Rodant), taxi/ride-hail (Drive Taxis Cardiff, Faircab, Green Taxi Cooperative, Kerala Taxi, People's Ride), food (Open Food Network Colombia/Italia, Katuma) and creative/tech (Ampled, Loconomics, Fairmondo, FairCoop). Notes that in skill-heavy sectors like software development, little capital is needed.
  - Literature summarised in the same paper: Casilli (2025, after Grohmann) on "dead platform co-ops" — weak organisational culture, scarce resources, poor planning, high turnover, limited user bases, **costly technology**, coordination problems in distributed workforces; Schneider (2018) — limited financing, market entry, awareness, competition.
  - **Borkin, Lloyd & Symons, "Platform co-operatives: solving the capital conundrum" (Nesta/Co-operatives UK, Feb 2019)** — capital is the primary obstacle: VC is incompatible with member control, and co-ops "don't have comparable mechanisms for risk taking/sharing or for converting anticipated future profit flows into present value." Context: UK community shares raised >£150M across 500+ enterprises from 150k+ investors. Proposed a £1M Platform Co-op Fund, tailored accelerators, and a "mutual shares" model.
  - **USFWC/DAWI State of the Sector 2025** (see above): even for ordinary worker co-ops, 36% cite "keeping up with administrative activities" and 39% marketing as top support needs.
- **Tech role:** Technology appears as a second-order cause: capital is the binding constraint, and building + maintaining an app is the main thing capital is needed for (plus marketing to reach network-effect scale). Regulatory app certification is a distinct tech barrier in taxis.
- **Status:** Euricse is the most recent (Dec 2025); a follow-up survey of the inactive co-ops was launched but no results found yet.
- **Sources:** https://euricse.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/WP-138_Tortorici_2025.12.18_FV.pdf ; https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/platform-co-operatives/ (Feb 2019) ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213297X22000039 ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352641478_Challenges_and_Success_Potentials_of_Platform_Cooperatives_Insights_from_a_Multiple_Case_Study ; https://eohub.rutgers.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-STATE-OF-THE-SECTOR-2.pdf

## Notes
**Recurring technology barriers (in order of how often they appear above):**
1. **Building and then maintaining the core app with no capital.** Drivers Co-op (founder's $400k personal guarantee); Resonate (closed 2024, repo idle since Jan 2023); Driver's Seat (app went stale, handed to a university); Drivers Co-op Colorado (suspended Apr 2025 over app problems). Launch is survivable; ongoing maintenance against moving platforms/OS/app stores is what kills.
2. **Regulatory/compliance software you don't choose:** TLC licensing and certified fare calculation for ride-hail (Euricse quote); Medicaid EVV with provider-paid systems and billing clearinghouses for home care (CHCA); FSMA 204 traceability records (KDEs/CTEs, 24-hour electronic spreadsheet) for food, enforcement from July 2028.
3. **Admin back-office load** — invoicing, payroll, social-security/tax filings, benefits. Smart's whole model is absorbing it; 36% of US worker co-ops name it as a top need.
4. **Marketing / reaching network-effect scale** against incumbents with marketing budgets (39% need marketing; Euricse's second-ranked challenge).
5. **Hosting and sysadmin skills** — even co-op tooling (Co-op Cloud) assumes CLI and container skills and calls itself beta.

**What worked:** a shared stack run by a federation or tech co-op and reused by many small local co-ops — CoopCycle (one codebase, 2% of revenue), Up & Go (CoLab-built, replicated to Philadelphia/Detroit), Smart (central admin platform), Co-op Cloud (shared recipes); or white-labelling a commercial stack for a flat fee (Colorado on TADA, ~$0.60/ride). Tech worker co-ops (CoLab, Autonomic, Hypha, Outlandish) already form an informal "co-op that builds for co-ops" layer, but on grant/client funding.

**Industries/work types that show up most:** (1) ride-hail and bike/courier delivery — the most attempts, and the most failures in Euricse's list; (2) care work — home care, cleaning, childcare; Medicaid-dependent and heavy on compliance; (3) freelance/creative — Smart, Stocksy, Resonate; (4) food/agriculture — food hubs, Open Food Network, farm co-ops facing FSMA 204; (5) data/gig-worker data co-ops — few, fragile. The workforce is mostly low-wage, immigrant and women workers with no time for tech work (Workers Lab: 60+ hour weeks). Co-op developers (Start.coop, Project Equity, DAWI, PCC) mostly offer coaching, legal templates, financing and research, not operating software. The only AI tooling found so far is advice chatbots (BCCM Co-op Buddy, Feb 2025) and feasibility studies.

**Gaps/uncertainties:** no CHCA-specific source on its scheduling/billing stack; no reconciled current member counts for CoopCycle or Drivers Co-op; no measured FSMA 204 cost for co-ops/food hubs specifically; no named co-op found bootstrapping software with AI coding agents.
