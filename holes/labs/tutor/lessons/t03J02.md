# Lesson 3: t03J02, gluing along a wild curve

**Problem.** Glue two tori along null-homotopic simple closed curves.  Compute `π₁` and homology by
van Kampen and Mayer-Vietoris.

The informal solution thickens each curve to an annulus.  For a smooth curve that is free.  The
registered statement allows any embedded circle, and thickening one to an annulus is Schoenflies.

## Moves

- **Ask what the tool needs.**  Van Kampen and Mayer-Vietoris need an open cover with known homotopy
  types.  They do not need that cover on `X`.  Any space homotopy equivalent to `X` has the same
  answers.  So the question moves from "find a collar" to "find a better model of `X`".
- **Build the model where the cover is a coordinate.**  Put the left torus at height -1, the right at
  1, and the circle at all heights between, inside `X × ℝ`.  Now the cover is `s < 1/2` and
  `s > -1/2`, and each homotopy changes `s` linearly.
- **Separate what the curve must give.**  Only one fact about the curve is left: a deformation `H` of
  the torus fixing the curve, ending in the curve on a neighbourhood.  Flatness of the torus and
  Tietze give that.  A wild curve does no harm because the deformation may leave the neighbourhood.
- **Do it in two stages.**  Moving the point and changing the height at once needs a reparametrisation
  that is not continuous on the curve.  Move the point first at fixed height, then change the height.

## Why the Schoenflies rows differ

t01J01, t00A03 and t00J05 ask for a homeomorphism to a standard surface.  Homotopy equivalent models
cannot give that.  Read the conclusion before pricing the foundation.

## Exercises

1. Show `Cyl` is homotopy equivalent to the double mapping cylinder of `T ← S¹ → T`.
2. Why can the deformation not simply be restricted to a neighbourhood to give a collar?
3. Where is null-homotopy used?  What changes for two essential curves?
4. The retraction exists only where the Tietze extension `G` is nonzero.  Why is that enough?
